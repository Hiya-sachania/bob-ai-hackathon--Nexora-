# Problem Statement

## Background
Clinical trials are an important part of developing and testing new medicines. They involve many patients, visits, and clinical trial sites.
During a trial, every patient visit has to follow certain rules given in the study protocol. These rules can include the correct medicine dose, visit dates, required tests, and allowed medicines.
When a trial has many patients and sites, checking all this information manually becomes difficult. Small mistakes or rule violations can be missed and may only be noticed later during an audit. This creates a need for a system that can continuously check the data and help risk managers find problems early.


## The Problem
In a large clinical trial, thousands of patient visits happen across many different sites. It is difficult to manually check every visit and make sure that all the protocol rules are being followed.
Problems such as missed visits, wrong doses, prohibited medicines, or missing tests may not be noticed immediately. If these problems are found only during a later audit, they can cause delays and increase the risk of the trial.
The main problem is that risk managers need a simple way to automatically find these deviations, understand how serious they are, and quickly identify which sites need the most attention.


## Who is Affected
Currently, clinical trial data is often checked through manual reviews, regular monitoring, and audits. Risk managers have to go through a large
amount of patient and site data to find protocol deviations. These methods can take time and may not identify problems as soon as they happen.
They also make it difficult to quickly compare many sites and find which ones are becoming high risk.
Because of this, important deviations may only be noticed during a later review or audit.
A system that continuously checks the data and highlights high-risk sites can help solve this problem.

## Why It Matters
This problem matters because missing protocol deviations can affect the safety and quality of a clinical trial. When problems are found late, the trial may need extra investigation and corrective action.
In a large trial with 5,000+ patient visits across 200+ sites, even small issues can become difficult to manage. A rejected submission can delay drug approval by 6–12 months and may cost around $50–100 million.
Our system helps find important problems earlier so that risk managers can take action before they become bigger issues.
